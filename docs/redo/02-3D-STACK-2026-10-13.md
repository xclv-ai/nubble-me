> **Direction changed 2026-10-13 (owner):** the matte-black toy direction (`03-CREATIVE-DIRECTION`) is withdrawn. The new target is POKPOK's painted and linocut style from `~/dev/pokpok-ai/marketing/pokpok-cd`, with buttons as physical objects wired together by open cables, still 3D so the gyroscope moves the shadows. ## 1. TL;DR

1. **Engine: RealityKit in a SwiftUI `RealityView` (`.virtual` camera) plus RealityKit's own `PostProcessEffect` hook.** The hook is **iOS 26**, so it works on the owner's phone today (checked in the SDK). The hook hands a Metal shader the finished frame's **colour + depth + projection matrix**. That is enough for every part of the look:
   - colour bands
   - hard shadow shapes
   - ink edges with chatter
   - paper or canvas grain
   - painterly smoothing
2. **The look comes from the renderer, not the textures.** Props are simple low-poly shapes in flat palette colours, lit by one shadow-casting key light. One full-screen pass then does the rest:
   - snaps every pixel to the nearest of about 12 palette tones (3 per colour) through a small lookup texture. This gives flat bands and a hard-edged shadow, and makes a fourth colour impossible.
   - draws dark teal ink where depth or the tone band jumps, with a noise-jittered width (the gouge)
   - multiplies paper tooth (linocut mode) or canvas plus a brush-patch filter (painted mode)

   **One shader, two modes.**
3. **iOS 27 stops mattering.** The painted/linocut look wants **hard** shadow shapes, which iOS 26 shadow maps already give. The soft-shadow, bloom and tone-mapping features that were iOS 27-only in the earlier version of this file are no longer needed.
4. **Cables: our own small rope simulation, not the physics engine.**
   - Each cable is 20–30 points with distance constraints (verlet), solved in the same per-frame update as everything else.
   - Gravity is the **real gravity from the phone** (relative to a slow rest reference), so cables swing and sag when you tilt, then settle.
   - Each cable is drawn as a tube through `LowLevelMesh` (iOS 18), rewritten every frame. It casts a real shadow.
   - A "tug" pulls a cable's end a few millimetres before an action fires.
   - A yellow pulse travels along it to the object that does the work: the owner's "transparent functionality".

   Cost is under 0.1 ms of CPU.
5. **Assets: three routes, use two.**
   - **(a) Low-poly props in flat palette colours (default, for everything that moves or is touched).** Many can be built in code from RealityKit primitives with no install. A model editor like Blender needs the owner's OK.
   - **(b) Painted backdrop plates (Seedream renders) for walls and far set pieces that never need to react.** The 3D props sit in front, so the gyroscope gives real parallax and real shadows on the floor.
   - **(c) Projecting Seedream renders onto geometry: only for one hero prop seen from the front.** It survives the ±12° orbit, but its painted-in shadows fight the moving real light.
6. **Three.js** (already planned for the browser prototype) has the easiest toon tools: `MeshToonMaterial` gradient maps, shadow maps, `EffectComposer` passes, rope code everywhere. **Inside the app it is capped at 60 fps by WebKit** (bug 294338: WKWebView renders near 60 fps unless the user flips a Safari setting). Use it to **find the look with the owner**. The shader math (lookup texture, edges, paper) ports almost line-for-line from GLSL to Metal.
7. **SceneKit** has the most direct toon hooks (`SCNShaderModifierEntryPointLightingModel` for banded light per light, `SCNTechnique` for multi-pass post, `preferredFramesPerSecond` to cap at 60). It is soft-deprecated (WWDC25). Only critical fixes; a dead end for a new app.
8. **CoreMotion is unchanged** (§6): gravity from `CMDeviceMotion`, a slow rest reference, clamp, per-frame spring. No permission needed. The camera orbits ±12° while the key light stays fixed, so shadows slide across the floor.

## 2. The painted / linocut shader: how it works and what it costs

### 2.1 Why post-process and not materials
- RealityKit's `CustomMaterial` (iOS, with Metal surface shaders) offers lighting models `lit / unlit / clearcoat` (checked). Its surface shader runs **before** lighting and **cannot read the light direction or the shadow map**, so it cannot quantise light into bands.
- `ShaderGraphMaterial` (MaterialX from Reality Composer Pro, iOS 18) has the same limit.
- The banding therefore happens **after** lighting: RealityKit renders normal lit flat-colour props with a real shadow map, and the post pass turns that image into bands. Because the palette is fixed, this is exact rather than an approximation.

### 2.2 The pass, step by step (one Metal compute kernel, full resolution)
| Step | What it does | Reads per pixel | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Tone snap | Look up the rendered colour in a **32×32×32 lookup texture** that maps every RGB to the nearest of about 12 palette tones (teal / pink / yellow / ink-black, each light / mid / shadow, plus paper). **Calibrate it from a swatch render**: render each palette colour under the key light on device, record the three bands, then bake the lookup. RealityKit's own colour grading is then absorbed automatically. | 1 | both |
| 2. Ink edges | Sobel on **depth** (object outlines) + "tone index changes" (creases of flat-shaded low-poly faces, and the shadow's edge). Line width is modulated by a 2D noise keyed to **world position**, reconstructed from depth + `projection`, so chatter sticks to objects while the camera orbits. | ~9 | linocut: 1.5–2.5 px dark teal; painted: thin, low opacity |
| 3. Paper tooth | Multiply a tiling paper texture **in screen space**. Paper is the print medium, so it should not move with the scene. Add ink-coverage noise so flat areas show pin-holes, and a 1–2 px offset of the pink layer for mis-registration. | 2 | linocut |
| 4. Brush patches | **Kuwahara filter** (4 sectors, radius 3–4) at **half resolution** on the tone-snapped image, giving painterly patches. Plus a canvas texture. Brush direction can come from a stroke texture mapped **in object space** (tri-planar in a `CustomMaterial` surface shader) so strokes stay on surfaces, not on the glass. | ~50–80 at half res | painted |
| 5. Yellow light | Yellow emissive objects (scanner bar, mic, cable pulse) skip the shadow band and get a 1-tone halo. That is the "one yellow light" of the references. | 1–4 | both |

**Cost (estimates, not measured; measure with the Metal HUD on day 1):**

| Mode | Estimated GPU time | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Linocut (steps 1, 2, 3, 5) at 1179×2556 | about 0.5–1 ms | 120 fps, with RealityKit's own frame at about 3–4 ms for a scene this small |
| Painted (steps 1, 2, 4, 5) | about 2–4 ms | Safe at **60 fps**. At 120 fps, run step 4 at quarter resolution or update it every other frame. |

### 2.3 What the post pass cannot do
- **No normals** in the hook: crease lines come from tone-band changes. That works because the props are flat-shaded low-poly, so each facet lands in its own band.
- **Shadow softness:** shadow maps on iOS 26 are hard. That is right for linocut. For "painted", the Kuwahara step softens the edge into a brush edge.
- **Transparency** (the scanner glass, glass bottles): handled as flat tone plus a painted highlight shape in the material, not as real refraction.

### 2.4 The same thing in each stack
| | RealityKit (chosen) | SceneKit | Custom Metal | Three.js (prototype) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Banded light | post lookup texture (exact for a fixed palette) | `.lightingModel` shader modifier, per light, before shadows are combined | anything | `MeshToonMaterial` + `gradientMap` (built in) |
| Hard shadows | `DirectionalLightComponent.Shadow` (fixed projection fitted to the stage) | `castsShadow`, `shadowMode` | write it | `shadowMap` (built in) |
| Ink edges | post, depth + tone index | `SCNTechnique` pass with depth/normal | anything | `OutlinePass` / custom Sobel `ShaderPass` |
| Paper / canvas / Kuwahara | post compute | `SCNTechnique` | anything | `ShaderPass` |
| Frame-rate cap | **none** on `RealityView` | `preferredFramesPerSecond` | yours | rAF, **60 fps in WKWebView** |
| Future | active | soft-deprecated | yours to maintain | fine on the web, second runtime in the app |
| Effort | low–medium | low now, dead end | high (renderer, shadows, animation, hit-testing, all yours) | lowest to iterate |

## 3. Cables as ropes: in each stack

### 3.1 Design (the same everywhere)
- **Verlet chain:** 24 points per cable (spacing ≈ cable length / 23). Each frame: integrate (`p += (p − pPrev)·damping + g·dt²`), then 6 rounds of distance constraints, then collisions.
- **Pinned ends:** one end on the button (plug), the other on the thing it powers (socket, device). Endpoints follow their objects every frame.
- **Collision:** the floor (`y ≥ radius`) and a few spheres for nearby props. Enough for "lying on the floor and coiling".
- **Gravity = the phone's.** `g_scene = normalize(down + 1.0·(gravity − slowReference))`, rotated into the scene frame. Tilt and the cables swing toward real down, then settle back as the reference catches up. Same rest logic as the camera (§6).
- **Tug before an action:**
  1. On press, the button-end anchor moves 6–8 mm away from the cable over 90 ms (the wire tightens), with a rigid haptic.
  2. Release.
  3. A **yellow pulse** (per-vertex colour or a UV offset in the material) runs along the tube in about 250 ms.
  4. The target object reacts when the pulse arrives.

  The user sees which wire carries the press to which machine.
- **Reduce Motion:** no gyro swing and no idle sway (gravity stays straight down). The tug becomes a pulse only.
- **Cost:** 8 cables × 24 points × 6 rounds is about 1,200 constraint solves a frame, well under 0.1 ms of CPU.

### 3.2 Per stack
| Stack | Simulation | Drawing |
|---|---|---|
| **RealityKit** | Our verlet in the same `System.update(context:)`. `PhysicsJointsComponent` + `PhysicsSphericalJoint` (iOS 18) can chain capsule bodies into a rope, but long chains jitter and are hard to "tug" precisely, so we keep our own. | **`LowLevelMesh`** (iOS 18, checked): one tube per cable (6 sides × 24 rings = 144 vertices), rewritten each frame through `withUnsafeMutableBytes(bufferIndex:)`. Make the mesh once with `MeshResource(from: lowLevelMesh)`. It casts and receives the shadow map, and the post pass gives it ink edges like everything else. |
| SceneKit | Our verlet, or `SCNPhysicsBallSocketJoint` chains | `SCNGeometry` rebuilt per frame, or a chain of capsule nodes |
| Three.js | Our verlet (many examples) | `TubeGeometry` rebuilt, or a custom `BufferGeometry` updated in place |
| Custom Metal | Our verlet, or a compute shader | Our tube vertex buffer |

| Option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **RealityKit + `PostProcessEffect`** | **Chosen** | It does everything the look needs on iOS 26:
  - post hook with colour + depth
  - hard shadow maps
  - per-frame `System`
  - `LowLevelMesh` for cables
  - SwiftUI-native entity tap targeting

  No dependency, and Apple's live engine. Gap: no frame-rate cap (handled in §7). |
| Three.js in the browser | **Use for the look prototype** (brief step 4) | Fastest loop with the owner on his phone. Shaders port to Metal. Do not ship it inside WKWebView as the main world: 60 fps cap, a second runtime, context loss on backgrounding, motion bridged from native. |
| SceneKit | No | Best toon hooks, but soft-deprecated (WWDC25 session 288; Apple's "Bringing your SceneKit projects to RealityKit"). |
| Custom Metal | No | A renderer, shadows, picking and animation from scratch for a look RealityKit plus one pass already reaches. |
| Unity as a Library | No | URP toon/outline assets exist, but a second runtime, binary size, UIKit hosting and seat licensing above the revenue threshold are too much for one scene. |
| Spline native runtime | No | Lights and shadows exist, but there is no custom post pass for the ink/paper look and no per-frame rope control. |
| Rive | Reduce Motion / widget fallback only | 2D vector, no real light or shadow. A good fit for a static linocut-style icon set. |

## 6. Gyroscope (unchanged from the first version)

- **Signal:** `CMDeviceMotion.gravity` with `startDeviceMotionUpdates(using: .xArbitraryZVertical, to:)`.
  - No magnetometer, no compass prompt, no yaw drift.
  - **No permission or Info.plist key** is needed (`NSMotionUsageDescription` is only for activity and pedometer).
  - SwiftUI has no motion API in SDK 27: `_CoreMotion_SwiftUI.framework` is an empty overlay (checked).
- **Rest pose:**
  - `refSlow += (g − refSlow) × 0.008` per 60 Hz sample, so a phone held still returns to the front view in about 2 s
  - `stageTilt = clamp((g − refSlow) × 3, −1…1)`
  - the text effects keep their faster reference (0.045, from `docs/EFFECTS-RESEARCH-2026-10-13.md` §4.3)

  **One shared `MotionLight` service** publishes both outputs plus the cable gravity vector.
- **Smoothing:** a 0.25 low-pass on the sensor, then a **critically damped spring in the per-frame `System`**, so 60 Hz samples give smooth 120 Hz motion. Publish to SwiftUI only on changes > 0.002.
- **Mapping:**

  | Input | Drives |
  |---|---|
  | `stageTilt.x` | camera yaw ±12° |
  | `stageTilt.y` | camera elevation (base 8°) ±6° |
  | — | the **key light stays fixed in the world**, so the hard shadow shapes slide across the floor in view |
  | slow-relative gravity | the cables |
  | the same light direction | SwiftUI shadows on any 2D cards, so the 2D and 3D layers agree |
- **Orientation:** remap the axes for landscape left/right; the app supports both.
- **Stop updates:** off screen, `scenePhase != .active`, Low Power Mode, Reduce Motion.
- **Reduce Motion:** no orbit, no cable swing, no idle sway. Presses still show the pulse along the wire, and nothing large moves.

```swift
// PrintLook.swift: the post pass
import RealityKit
import Metal

struct PrintLook: PostProcessEffect {
    enum Mode { case linocut, painted }
    let mode: Mode
    // Pipelines + textures created in prepare(for:)
    nonisolated(unsafe) static var snapEdgePaper: MTLComputePipelineState?
    nonisolated(unsafe) static var lut: MTLTexture?, paper: MTLTexture?

    func prepare(for device: MTLDevice) {
        let lib = device.makeDefaultLibrary()!
        Self.snapEdgePaper = try? device.makeComputePipelineState(function: lib.makeFunction(name: "printLook")!)
        // load palette_lut (3D, 32³) and paper_tooth via MTKTextureLoader …
    }

    func postProcess(context: borrowing PostProcessEffectContext<any MTLCommandBuffer>) {   // VERIFY exact requirement name in the protocol
        guard let enc = context.commandBuffer.makeComputeCommandEncoder(),
              let pso = Self.snapEdgePaper else { return }
        enc.setComputePipelineState(pso)
        enc.setTexture(context.sourceColorTexture, index: 0)
        enc.setTexture(context.sourceDepthTexture, index: 1)
        enc.setTexture(Self.lut, index: 2)
        enc.setTexture(Self.paper, index: 3)
        enc.setTexture(context.targetColorTexture, index: 4)
        var inv = context.projection.inverse
        enc.setBytes(&inv, length: MemoryLayout<simd_float4x4>.size, index: 0)
        let w = pso.threadExecutionWidth, h = pso.maxTotalThreadsPerThreadgroup / w
        enc.dispatchThreads(MTLSize(width: context.targetColorTexture.width,
                                    height: context.targetColorTexture.height, depth: 1),
                            threadsPerThreadgroup: MTLSize(width: w, height: h, depth: 1))
        enc.endEncoding()
        // painted mode: a second half-res Kuwahara dispatch before this one
    }
}
```

```metal
// PrintLook.metal (linocut core)
kernel void printLook(texture2d<half, access::read>   src   [[texture(0)]],
                      depth2d<float, access::read>     depth [[texture(1)]],
                      texture3d<half, access::sample>  lut   [[texture(2)]],
                      texture2d<half, access::sample>  paper [[texture(3)]],
                      texture2d<half, access::write>   dst   [[texture(4)]],
                      constant float4x4& invProj [[buffer(0)]],
                      uint2 p [[thread_position_in_grid]]) {
    constexpr sampler s(filter::nearest, address::clamp_to_edge);
    constexpr sampler rep(filter::linear, address::repeat);
    half3 c   = src.read(p).rgb;
    half4 ink = lut.sample(s, float3(c));                  // rgb = palette tone, a = tone index / 15
    // Edge: depth discontinuity OR tone-index change among the 4 neighbours
    float d = depth.read(p);
    float dz = 0; half di = 0;
    for (int2 o : {int2(1,0), int2(-1,0), int2(0,1), int2(0,-1)}) {
        uint2 q = uint2(clamp(int2(p) + o, int2(0), int2(dst.get_width()-1, dst.get_height()-1)));
        dz = max(dz, abs(depth.read(q) - d));
        di = max(di, abs(lut.sample(s, float3(src.read(q).rgb)).a - ink.a));
    }
    // Gouge chatter: width noise keyed to view-space position (sticks to objects while orbiting)
    float4 v = invProj * float4(float2(p) / float2(dst.get_width(), dst.get_height()) * 2 - 1, d, 1);
    float chatter = fract(sin(dot(floor(v.xy / v.w * 400.0), float2(12.9898, 78.233))) * 43758.5453);
    bool edge = (dz > 0.002 || di > 0.03) && chatter > 0.18;
    half3 col = edge ? half3(0.04, 0.17, 0.19) : ink.rgb;  // dark teal ink
    // Paper tooth in screen space: ink sits on top, tooth shows through flat areas
    half t = paper.sample(rep, float2(p) / 512.0).r;
    col *= mix(half(1.0), t, half(0.35));
    dst.write(half4(col, 1), p);
}
```

```swift
    var p: [SIMD3<Float>], prev: [SIMD3<Float>]          // 24 points
    var segment: Float
    var anchorA: Entity, anchorB: Entity                  // plug end, socket end
    var tug: Float = 0                                    // 0…1, set on press
    var pulse: Float = -1                                 // 0…1 along the cable, <0 = none
    let mesh: LowLevelMesh                                // tube, 6 sides × 24 rings
}

@MainActor struct CableSystem: System {
    static let q = EntityQuery(where: .has(CableComponent.self))
    init(scene: Scene) {}
    func update(context: SceneUpdateContext) {
        let dt = Float(min(context.deltaTime, 1.0 / 30))
        let g = MotionLight.shared.cableGravity * 9.8      // slow-relative real gravity, scene frame
        for e in context.entities(matching: Self.q, updatingSystemWhen: .rendering) {
            guard var c = e.components[CableComponent.self] else { continue }
            let n = c.p.count
            for i in 1..<(n - 1) {                         // integrate
                let v = (c.p[i] - c.prev[i]) * 0.985
                c.prev[i] = c.p[i]; c.p[i] += v + g * dt * dt
            }
            let pull = normalize(c.p[1] - c.p[0]) * (-0.007 * c.tug)   // tug: anchor pulls 7 mm
            c.p[0] = c.anchorA.position(relativeTo: nil) + pull
            c.p[n - 1] = c.anchorB.position(relativeTo: nil)
            for _ in 0..<6 {                               // distance constraints + floor
                for i in 0..<(n - 1) {
                    let d = c.p[i + 1] - c.p[i], len = length(d), k = (len - c.segment) / max(len, 1e-5) * 0.5
                    if i > 0 { c.p[i] += d * k }
                    if i + 1 < n - 1 { c.p[i + 1] -= d * k }
                }
                for i in 1..<(n - 1) { c.p[i].y = max(c.p[i].y, 0.004) }
            }
            c.tug = max(0, c.tug - dt / 0.09)
            if c.pulse >= 0 { c.pulse += dt / 0.25; if c.pulse > 1 { c.pulse = -1 /* fire target */ } }
            c.mesh.withUnsafeMutableBytes(bufferIndex: 0) { raw in
                writeTube(points: c.p, radius: 0.003, pulse: c.pulse, into: raw)   // positions, normals, pulse colour
            }
            e.components.set(c)
        }
    }
}
```

`StageSystem` is the camera orbit from the first version of this file:
- critically damped spring toward `MotionLight.shared.stageTilt`
- `camera.look(at: pivot, from: pivot + dist·(sin yaw·cos pitch, sin pitch, cos yaw·cos pitch))`
- zero with Reduce Motion

## 10. Two-day build plan

Precondition: the owner has picked the mode (linocut or painted) in the **Three.js browser prototype** (brief step 4), using the same lookup-texture, edge and paper shaders in GLSL, the same gyro constants and the same cable code. The native build ports those.

**Day 1: the world, printed**
1. `WorldScene` built from primitives (route A1): floor, one backdrop plate (route B, a Seedream render in palette), a key light with a fixed tight shadow, three physical buttons (rocker, push, dial), a socket panel.
2. `PrintLook` linocut mode: calibrate the lookup texture from an on-device swatch render, then ink edges and paper.
3. Device checks, each with a screenshot or HUD number:
   - the hook's input pixel format
   - post-pass GPU ms
   - fps at 120 Hz
   - is the shadow shape hard and banded?
   - do the edges chatter but stay put while orbiting?

**Day 2: alive and wired**
1. `MotionLight` (fast and slow outputs + cable gravity, orientation remap, Low Power / Reduce Motion stops) and `StageSystem` orbit.
2. `CableSystem` with `LowLevelMesh` tubes: sag, gyro swing, floor contact; tug → pulse → target reacts; haptics.
3. Painted mode (Kuwahara at half res + canvas). Measure; choose 60 or 120 fps per mode.
4. Off-screen teardown, thermal step-down, still placeholder. Record a screen video on the iPhone 15 Pro for the owner.

**Done when:**
- A screenshot reads as a linocut print (or a painting) in the four palette colours, with no fourth colour.
- Tilting slides the hard shadows and swings the cables, which settle when the phone is held still.
- A press visibly tugs a wire and sends a pulse to the thing that acts.
- The HUD holds the chosen frame rate for 2 minutes without thermal state `.serious`.
- Reduce Motion stops all swinging.

## 11. Open checks and risks
1. **VERIFY** the exact `PostProcessEffect` requirement name and signature (the protocol's method was not printed in the grep). Also whether `sourceColorTexture` is linear HDR or display-ready, since the lookup-texture calibration depends on it.
2. Tone snapping is exact only while every surface is a palette colour. A Seedream backdrop with off-palette pixels gets snapped too, which is usually good (palette law) but can posterise gradients harshly. Generate plates already in palette.
3. Kuwahara cost at 120 fps is an estimate; painted mode may need to be a 60 fps mode.
4. Installing Blender (route A2) needs the owner's go.
5. WKWebView 60 fps cap: WebKit bug 294338, "Allow WKWebView to Support 120Hz Refresh Rate on ProMotion Devices", still open.
