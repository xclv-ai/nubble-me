## A. Apple Human Interface Guidelines — 12 rules

**A1. Do give every modal and every full-screen view an exit in the place iOS users already look, like this:** the round-of-three and the article reader get a Close button in the top toolbar *and* swipe-down; the tab bar stays visible underneath everything that is not modal.
Source: [Modality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality) — "Always give people an obvious way to dismiss a modal view. In general, it works well to follow the platform conventions people already know. For example, in iOS, iPadOS, and watchOS apps, people typically expect to find a button in the top toolbar or swipe down." · [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars) — "Make sure the tab bar is visible when people navigate to different sections of your app. If you hide the tab bar, people can forget which area of the app they're in. The exception is when a modal view covers the tab bar, because a modal is temporary and self-contained."

**A2. Do use full-screen only for one scoped, multistep task with its controls still reachable, like this:** the daily round may fill the screen (three steps, one path), but the exit and the "2 of 3" progress stay on screen; the article depth levels are a sheet, not a second full-screen stack.
Source: [Modality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality) — "Consider using a full-screen modal style for in-depth content or a complex task." and "Take care to avoid creating a modal experience that feels like an app within your app. … If a modal task must contain subviews, provide a single path through the hierarchy and avoid including buttons that people might mistake for the button that dismisses the modal view." · [Going full screen](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/going-full-screen) — "Continue to provide access to essential features and controls so people can complete their task without exiting full-screen mode." and "Let people choose when to exit full-screen mode."

**A3. Do pair every Done with a Cancel/Back, and let sheets swipe away, like this:** the ℞ sheet has "Keep" (trailing) and "Not now" (leading), a grabber, and a medium detent that shows the verdict first.
Source: [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets) — "Provide an alternative to the Done button. If you provide a Done button, always pair it with a Cancel button … Relying solely on the Done button implies that completing the task is the only way to exit the sheet, which can feel restrictive or misleading." · "Support swiping to dismiss a sheet." · "Include a grabber in a resizable sheet." · "In an iPhone app, consider supporting the medium detent to allow progressive disclosure of the sheet's content."

**A4. Do order by importance top-to-leading and disclose progressively, like this:** the signal sheet shows headline, then verdict, then ℞; depth levels 01–04 open on demand, never stacked on the first screen.
Source: [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout) — "Order content by relative importance. People often start by viewing content in reading order — that is, from top to bottom and from the leading to trailing side — so place the most important items near the top and leading side of the window or display." · "Use progressive disclosure to make layouts cleaner and easier to interact with. An interface with too much content and too many choices makes it harder to find information quickly, and harder to understand the choices that are available."

**A5. Do use the system text styles with body at 17 pt, nothing under 11 pt, no light weights, three sizes per screen, like this:** Large Title 34 (headline), Body 17 (verdict/text), Caption 1 12 (meta) — nothing else on a signal screen.
Source: [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography) — iOS "Default size 17 pt / Minimum size 11 pt"; "In general, avoid light font weights. For example, if you're using system-provided fonts, prefer Regular, Medium, Semibold, or Bold font weights, and avoid Ultralight, Thin, and Light font weights"; "Minimize the number of typefaces you use, even in a highly customized interface. Mixing too many different typefaces can obscure your information hierarchy and hinder readability". Large (default) Dynamic Type table: Large Title 34/41 · Title 1 28/34 · Title 2 22/28 · Title 3 20/25 · Headline 17/22 Semibold · Body 17/22 · Callout 16/21 · Subhead 15/20 · Footnote 13/18 · Caption 1 12/16 · Caption 2 11/13.
And keep the hierarchy when text grows, letting the headline win, like this: at xxxLarge and AX sizes the headline stays at the top, the verdict wraps to as many lines as needed, the meta line is the one allowed to stay small.
Source: [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography) — "Prioritize important content when responding to text-size changes. Not all content is equally important." · "Maintain a consistent information hierarchy regardless of the current font size. For example, keep primary elements toward the top of a view even when the font size is very large" · "Keep text truncation to a minimum as font size increases." · [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) — "Ideally, give people the option to enlarge text by at least 200 percent".

**A6. Do meet contrast on both appearances with POKPOK's own colours, like this:** off-white #FDFDFC on teal #0A3B44 for body; pink #FFC4D6 on teal only at ≥18 pt or bold; yellow #F5F900 never carries small text — it carries the one action's background with black text.
Source: [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) — "Up to 17 pts, All, 4.5:1 · 18 pts, All, 3:1 · All, Bold, 3:1" and "If your app supports Dark Mode, make sure to check the minimum contrast in both light and dark appearances." · [Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode) — "At a minimum, make sure the contrast ratio between colors is no lower than 4.5:1. For custom foreground and background colors, strive for a contrast ratio of 7:1, especially in small text." · "Avoid offering an app-specific appearance setting."

**A7. Do keep Liquid Glass on the control layer and let colour mark one live thing, like this:** the floating tab bar and the system Close button are glass; the painted scene (floor, wall, copier, booth, sheets, tickets, cables) is matte content; yellow is the light — only the machine that is live now glows yellow and its cable is bright, every other cable is dim teal; one pink object per screen (the copier, the booth window); red only on the code-red bulb.
Source: [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials) — "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer." · "Use Liquid Glass effects sparingly. … Limit these effects to the most important functional elements in your app." · "Only use clear Liquid Glass for components that appear over visually rich backgrounds." (and: "If the underlying content is bright, consider adding a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity.") · [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color) — "Apply color sparingly to the Liquid Glass material, and to symbols or text on the material. … To emphasize primary actions, apply color to the background rather than to symbols or text. … Refrain from adding color to the background of multiple controls." · "Avoid using the same color to mean different things." · "Avoid relying solely on color to differentiate between objects, indicate interactivity, or communicate essential information."

**A8. Do add motion only where it carries meaning, keep it short, cancellable, and never the only channel, like this:** a sheet prints out of the tray in ~300 ms and is tappable while it moves; Dr HAOS's reaction plays once; the Close switch and the depth lever never animate idly.
Source: [Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion) — "Add motion purposefully, supporting the experience without overshadowing it. Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion." · "Make motion optional. … supplement visual feedback by also using alternatives like haptics and audio". · "Aim for brevity and precision in feedback animations." · "In apps, generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently." · "Let people cancel motion. As much as possible, don't make people wait for an animation to complete before they can do anything".

**A9. Do ship a Reduce Motion variant of the painted scene, like this:** gyroscope parallax of the painted scene off, camera fixed, sheets fade in instead of printing out, cables do not sway, no zooms, no blur transitions; the character still blinks and the scan bar still glows (a state, not a motion that moves the scene).
Source: [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) — "Be cautious with fast-moving and blinking animations. … When this setting is active, ensure your app or game responds by reducing automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion. Other best practices for reducing motion include: Tightening animation springs to reduce bounce effects · Tracking animations directly with people's gestures · Avoiding animating depth changes in z-axis layers · Replacing transitions in x-, y-, and z-axes with fades to avoid motion · Avoiding animating into and out of blurs". (visionOS note worth keeping for a breathing loop: [Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion) — "avoid showing an oscillation that has a frequency of around 0.2 Hz because people can be very sensitive to this frequency." A 2.2 s breath is 0.45 Hz, outside that band; keep it there.)

**A10. Do use depth to say what matters and never put depth on text, like this (visionOS guidance applied to a painted scene with a floor and a wall):** the machine in use stands forward on the floor, the others sit back against the wall; the headline is flat in the UI layer, never painted onto a machine at an angle; one depth change per interaction, not a constant parallax churn.
Source: [Spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout) — "Use depth to communicate hierarchy. Depth helps an object appear to stand out from surrounding content, making it more noticeable." · "In general, avoid adding depth to text. Text that appears to hover above its background is difficult to read" · "Make sure depth adds value. In general, you want to use depth to clarify and delight — you don't need to use it everywhere. … People need to refocus their eyes to perceive each difference in depth, and doing so too often or quickly can be tiring." · "Provide visual cues that accurately communicate the depth of your content."

**A11. Do give haptics one meaning each, match them to the animation's weight, keep them optional, and keep them away from the gyroscope, like this:** `.notification(.warning)` only for code red; `.impact(.rigid)` when a sheet drops into the tray or a switch throws; `.selection` when the depth level changes; a settings toggle; no haptic while the gyro camera is mid-orbit (play it at rest).
Source: [Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics) — "Use system-provided haptic patterns according to their documented meanings." · "Use haptics consistently throughout your app or game. It's important to build a clear, causal relationship between each haptic and the action that causes it" · "you generally want to match the intensity and sharpness of a haptic with the intensity and sharpness of the animation it accompanies." · "Avoid overusing haptics. … Often, the best haptic experience is one that people may not be conscious of, but miss when it's turned off." · "Make haptics optional." · "Be aware that playing haptics might impact other user experiences. … Ensure that haptic vibrations don't disrupt experiences involving device features like the camera, gyroscope, or microphone." Patterns: Notification Success/Warning/Error; Impact Light/Medium/Heavy/Rigid/Soft; Selection.
And treat sound as a bonus that obeys the silent switch, like this: Dr HAOS's short sounds (the tray click, the ℞ stamp, the mic pop) use the Ambient category, mix with the user's music, and are mirrored by a haptic so silent-mode users lose nothing.
Source: [Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio) — "When a device is in silent mode, it plays only the audio that people explicitly initiate" · Ambient category: "Responds to the silence switch. Mixes with other sounds. Doesn't play in the background." · "Use the system's sound services to play short sounds and vibrations." · [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback) — "Make sure all feedback is accessible. … when you provide feedback using color, text, sound, and haptics, people can receive it whether they silence their device, look away from the screen, or use VoiceOver."

**A12. Do launch straight into today, show something within a glance, keep onboarding to one interactive beat, and alert only when actionable, like this:** first launch = Dr HAOS, one line, one tap ("Add your competitors"); later launches restore the exact sheet the user left; a round that takes >1 s shows the yellow scan bar sweeping the copier glass — the light is the progress — never a spinner alone; no alert at launch, ever.
Source: [Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching) — "Launch instantly." · "Restore the previous state when your app restarts so people can continue where they left off." · "Design a launch screen that's nearly identical to the first screen of your app or game." · [Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding) — "Teach through interactivity." · "Consider providing a collection of context-specific tips instead of a single onboarding flow." · "Postpone nonessential setup flows or customization steps." · [Loading](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading) — "Show something as soon as possible. … consider showing placeholder text, graphics, or animations as content loads" · "If loading takes an unavoidably long time, give people something interesting to view while they wait." · [Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts) — "Use alerts sparingly." · "Avoid using an alert merely to provide information." · "Avoid showing an alert when your app starts." · [Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications) — "Avoid sending multiple notifications for the same thing, even if someone hasn't responded." · "Use a badge only to show people how many unread notifications they have."

## B. Game feel and character craft — 12 rules

**B1. Do stack many small reactions on one input, like this:** tapping a sheet = sound + 2-px camera kick toward it + rigid haptic + the sheet lifts 8 px + its cable goes taut + Dr HAOS's eyes cut to it + a 16 ms hold before the verdict text lands. Five reactions, one tap, ~250 ms total.
Source: Jan Willem Nijman (Vlambeer), [RPS interview](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/interview-jan-willem-nijman-on-nuclear-thrones-feel), the written version of the "art of screenshake" method — "We then kick the camera back 6 pixels from where you are aiming, and 'add 4 to the screenshake'. The screenshake degenerates quickly" · "The get hit animation always starts with a frame white, then two frames of the character looking hit with big eyes. The game also freezes for about 10-20 milliseconds whenever you hit something." · "It's the mix of things that matters, not the things themselves." The talk itself: [The Art of Screenshake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U), listed by [Kenney](https://kenney.nl/learn/must-see-videos-for-indie-developers) — "why is it that one game plays great and another similar one feels terrible?"

**B2. Do make sound the first reaction and give each prop its own voice, like this:** a signal sheet lands in the tray with a dry paper click; the code-red bulb comes on with a low filament thud; the ℞ gets a rubber stamp; the mic pops once when Dr HAOS speaks. Four sounds, all under 200 ms, all also haptic.
Source: Nijman, [RPS](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/interview-jan-willem-nijman-on-nuclear-thrones-feel) — "the most important factor would probably be sound. … A good, punchy, bassy gunshot does wonders." · "Hitting an enemy also … plays that enemies own specific impact sounds (which is a mix of a material – meat, plant, rock or metal – getting hit and that characters own hit sound)". ADA 2026 finalist receipt: [PowerWash Simulator](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/) — "thoughtful touches like specific haptic feedback for each hose nozzle."

**B3. Do leave permanence on the floor, like this:** a read signal stays on the floor as a sheet turned face-down; an accepted ℞ leaves its stamped ticket on the spike; the discharge screen shows today's three sheets where they fell, cables still plugged in. The day leaves evidence.
Source: Nijman, [RPS](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/interview-jan-willem-nijman-on-nuclear-thrones-feel) — "The props are there to add some permanence to the battles. We'd rather have a loose bullet flying and hitting a cactus (to show you that there has been a battle there) than for it to hit a wall."

**B4. Do make everything respond and make the response cascade, like this:** every painted object answers a tap (the bulb flickers, the bottle wobbles, Dr HAOS side-eyes); the one input "accept ℞" triggers cable tug + stamp + slow blink + the sheet turning over.
Source: "Juice it or lose it" (Jonasson & Purho), [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg), as listed by [Kenney](https://kenney.nl/learn/must-see-videos-for-indie-developers) — "A juicy game feels alive and responds to everything the player does" and "tons of cascading actions and response for minimal user input." · ADA 2026 Delight winner [Is This Seat Taken?](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=z12xq8fa) — "Tapping objects in each scene produces playful and unexpected responses, and the interface hides its own Easter eggs."

**B5. Do anticipate, follow through, and add one secondary action, like this:** before the ℞ ticket prints, its cable tugs and the stamp arm lifts (anticipation); when a sheet stops, its headline settles 60 ms after the sheet and the cable sways once (follow-through); accepting the ℞ gets a small puff of ink dust, not confetti (secondary action, in character).
Source: [IxDF, Disney's 12 principles applied to UI](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/ui-animation-how-to-apply-disney-s-12-principles-of-animation-to-ui-design) — "Anticipation helps to prepare the viewer for what's about to happen, and it's key to making any motion feel real." · "In UI animation design, you can have elements that are related to each other but that may move at different rates. … The initiator of the movement will be the most crucial element … Then the title and description follow." · "Secondary actions are used to support or emphasize the main action going on within a scene." · "A good animation effect will be invisible to the viewer. However, if you exaggerate too much, it may annoy users."

**B6. Do stage one motion at a time and ease everything, like this:** when a sheet is in focus, Dr HAOS holds still except for breath and blink; when he reacts, the machines do not; every move eases in and out (no linear tweens).
Source: [IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/ui-animation-how-to-apply-disney-s-12-principles-of-animation-to-ui-design) — "Use animation to show your user where to focus within the interface and keep the motion of everything else (that's not as important) to a minimum." · "Without easing in and out, your animations will feel robotic." · "How quickly or slowly an object moves gives us a lot of information."

**B7. Do build the character as a state machine with idle, reaction and resolution states that can be interrupted by the user, like this:** Idle (breath 2.2 s, blink 150/100 ms every 4–8 s, ear twitch), Scanning (the yellow bar sweeps while a round loads), Reacting (side-eye to the tapped machine), Resolution (slow blink on ℞ accepted; dead-eyes on error), Sleeping (discharged). If the user taps away mid-reaction, the reaction stops — he never finishes a speech nobody is watching.
Source: Duolingo, [Art meets technology](https://blog.duolingo.com/world-character-visemes/) — "Rive's State Machine: a visual representation of the logic that connects the animations ('states') together … it allowed us to programmatically control which animation states are called, how they are called, and how they transition and blend together." · "lets us react to what the user does in real time. When you click on a word, the character will say and animate that word; if you finish the exercise before it is done speaking, the character will stop speaking in time. We can also show idle behavior like head nods, blinking, and eyebrows moving. Finally, based on the outcome of the challenge – if you get it right or wrong – we can move to a final state, showing the reaction to your response!"

**B8. Do make the character mirror the person a little, like this:** eyes follow the finger (≤15% travel, per the mascot spec), the head turns toward the sheet the user is reading, and the gyroscope tilt moves the camera across the painted floor and wall so his shadow slides — the phone's own face-tracking precedent is the proof that mirroring reads as alive.
Source: Apple, [iPhone X press release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2017/09/the-future-is-here-iphone-x/) — "the TrueDepth camera captures and analyzes over 50 different facial muscle movements, then animates those expressions in a dozen different Animoji" · "iPhone X users can record and send Animoji messages that mirror their facial expressions."

**B9. Do tune the touch response for months, not the shading, like this:** the first week of the prototype is spent on the sheet tap, the depth lever and the stamp — the vibration under the finger, the ease curve, the 100 ms budget — before any texture work.
Source: ADA 2026 Interaction winner Moonlitt — Apple's [winners page](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/): "best-in-class Liquid Glass integration, and easy onboarding"; [Apple Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=v1nphz91), Pizzano: "with Liquid Glass everything felt much more alive. It was like the interface was breathing."; Mariniello: "How much complexity disappears into the experience. … the goal was never to make people feel the complexity." · [gori.me interview at WWDC26](https://gori.me/apps/iphone-app-news/167222) (Japanese): "スライダーを動かしたときの手応え、指先に返ってくる小さな振動、アニメーションの心地よさ。その調整だけで数カ月を費やした" — the feel of moving the slider, the small vibration returned to the fingertip, the pleasantness of the animation: they spent several months on that tuning alone.

**B10. Do let one hand-made detail carry the whole personality and strip everything else, like this:** Dr HAOS's verdict is printed onto the sheet line by line as the copier head passes (Instrument Serif italic, revealed like ink), the status bar area is painted as part of the wall; no login, no sync badge, no settings on the first screen.
Source: ADA 2026 Delight winner grug — Apple [winners page](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/): "Check out the app's hand-drawn status bar … there's no login, no cloud syncing, nothing extraneous — just a simple idea"; [Apple Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ux44ymcr), van der Velde: "it felt magical, not like a font fading in but like ink appearing on paper. It suddenly felt like grug was on the other side of the screen writing the lesson for you."; Elings: "If we had compromised on the handwritten engine early, grug would probably feel like a quote app with a cute font. Instead, it feels like its own little world." and "grug makes the morning feel a little lighter."

**B11. Do cut any mechanic the user does not ask for and put the fun in discovery, not in the task, like this:** no "drive the bus" — no swiping to sort signals, no rating the verdict; the user only reads, accepts or skips; the pleasure is in what Dr HAOS does and says around that.
Source: [Is This Seat Taken? Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=z12xq8fa), Dalmau: "'Driving is not fun.' In one of the early versions, you were the bus driver and could open and close the doors. … players didn't care for it. They only wanted to sort the characters. … We ended up removing it entirely." · "The fun wasn't necessarily in the puzzle itself, but in seeing different scenarios and discovering different interactions while solving the puzzle." · "We designed it by asking ourselves, 'Is this necessary? Does this add value to the game?' If the answer was no, we removed it." · Apple: "With no timers but lots of smiles".

**B12. Do make the data the picture and let the palette follow the day, like this:** the "170 stories read" is one full-width chart on the discharge screen; the wall's light shifts from morning off-white to evening teal; the headline figure is readable by someone who knows nothing.
Source: ADA 2026 Visuals winner Tide Guide — Apple [winners page](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/): "rich full-screen charts present a boatload of weather data … in a crisp, clear manner that's easily understood by even the most landlocked" and "The app's palette is even designed to match the color of the sky throughout the day"; [Apple profile](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=4r9b23wx), MacDonald: "I just needed to show current conditions." and "The more time you spend with it, the more you can dig into different layers."
Companion-app receipts for the "alive" checklist: Finch, [Pratt design critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/) — "Completing a task results in immediate visible responses: the Finch gains energy, performs joyful animations, or displays messages" and, as the failure to avoid, "Journaling entries, for example, sometimes fail to trigger animations, leaving users unsure whether their input was recorded. … mapping between action and system response must be reliable". Headspace, [Nexus Studios](https://nexusstudios.com/work/headspace-case-study/) — "over 150 unique characters across 80 minutes of bespoke animated in-app content", "visual analogies with genuine warmth". Tamagotchi effect, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagotchi_effect) — "the development of emotional attachment with machines, robots or software agents"; the original pet "would 'die' within half a day" if ignored — the attachment came from needs the user could meet, which is why Dr HAOS must visibly *change* when the user acts (slow blink, turned sheet), not merely talk.

## B-bis. Craft rules for painted machines and open wires — 7 rules

The owner's rule, verbatim: "all buttons in app could be like physical objects unconventionally, all connected with open wires. I need transparent functionality." These rules say how the craft rules above (anticipation, follow-through, secondary action, feedback, permanence) land on a control that is a painted machine with a visible yellow cable.

**B13. Do give every control a cable that visibly reaches the thing it changes, like this:** the ℞ stamp's yellow cable runs across the floor to the prescriptions drawer; the depth lever's cable runs into the copier; the competitor watch-list's cable runs up the wall to the red bulb; the mic's cable runs to Dr HAOS's socket. A control whose wire goes nowhere is a bug: the wire is the explanation.
Source: HIG [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback) — "Consider integrating status feedback into your interface. When status feedback is available near the items it describes, people get important information without having to take action or leave their current context." · NN/g, [Minimize Cognitive Load](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/minimize-cognitive-load/) — "Build on existing mental models" (a wire from a switch to a lamp is the oldest mental model of cause and effect) · [Finch critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/) — "mapping between action and system response must be reliable".

**B14. Do move the cable before the machine acts, like this:** tap "next signal" → the cable twitches and goes taut (≈80 ms, a 2-px lift along its length), the copier's scan bar lights, then the sheet prints. The tug is the anticipation; it tells the eye where to look before anything happens. Under Reduce Motion the cable only brightens.
Source: [IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/ui-animation-how-to-apply-disney-s-12-principles-of-animation-to-ui-design) — "Anticipation helps to prepare the viewer for what's about to happen, and it's key to making any motion feel real." · Nijman, [RPS](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/interview-jan-willem-nijman-on-nuclear-thrones-feel) — "when a shotgun reloads … we add some reverse weapon kick. This makes it look as if the character is reloading manually."

**B15. Do let the machine settle after the sheet stops, like this:** the sheet stops, the headline settles 60 ms later, the tray lid drops, the cable sways once and goes slack — in that order, each eased, none longer than 150 ms. Nothing in the scene snaps to rest.
Source: [IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/ui-animation-how-to-apply-disney-s-12-principles-of-animation-to-ui-design) — "This phenomenon is critical when objects come to a standstill after being in motion. … your hair might still be in motion slightly after you've stopped moving your head (follow through)." · "Without easing in and out, your animations will feel robotic."

**B16. Do give each machine its own three-channel reply and show it at the socket, like this:** the copier answers with a paper click + a rigid tap + the tray light; the stamp with a thud + a heavy tap + the ink mark; the mic with a pop + a soft tap + the booth window warming pink; and at the far end of every cable the socket's small lamp blinks once — the reply is visible at both ends of the wire.
Source: HIG [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback) — "Make sure all feedback is accessible. … color, text, sound, and haptics" · HIG [Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics) — "match the intensity and sharpness of a haptic with the intensity and sharpness of the animation it accompanies" · ADA 2026 finalist [PowerWash Simulator](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/) — "specific haptic feedback for each hose nozzle."

**B17. Do make yellow the light that shows what is powered, like this:** only the machine that is live now glows yellow (the scan bar while reading, the stamp when the ℞ is ready, the mic when Dr HAOS speaks); its cable is bright yellow, every other cable is dim teal; a sleeping machine has no light. A screen never has two things lit, so the founder always knows the one thing that is waiting for him.
Source: HIG [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color) — "Apply color sparingly … reserve it for elements that truly benefit from emphasis, such as status indicators or primary actions." · "Avoid using the same color to mean different things." · HIG [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) — "Convey information with more than color alone." (the lit machine also moves and sounds).

**B18. Do make the machine unconventional and the navigation conventional, like this:** the Close control may be a painted toggle switch and the depth control a lever on the copier, but the Close sits in the top toolbar where iOS users look and swipe-down still works; the tab bar is the system's; no painted object is the only way out of anything.
Source: HIG [Modality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality) — "Always give people an obvious way to dismiss a modal view. In general, it works well to follow the platform conventions people already know." · Tide Guide's maker, [Apple profile](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=4r9b23wx), on what he had to unlearn: "I had been creating apps that used crazy elements, non-standard navigation, stuff like that." · HIG [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) — "Offer alternatives to gestures. … if you use a swipe gesture to dismiss a view, also make a button available".

**B19. Do keep the machines hand-made and the sheets honest, like this:** brush texture, wobbly contours and a copier that prints a cup when a bottle lies on the glass are the style (that joke is POKPOK's own picture of the perception gap); but every number and name printed on a sheet in the app is the data, and a sheet never shows a placeholder or a design's sample text. The imperfection is in the paint, never in the facts.
Source: ADA 2026 [grug Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ux44ymcr) — "The imperfection is intentional. The roughness sets the stage for grug's character" · POKPOK's own style parameters, `marketing/pokpok-cd/STYLE-PARAMETERS.md` — "hand-painted matte texture, visible brush irregularity, paper grain, slightly wobbly hand-drawn contours … Sculpted, not rendered. No photorealism, no glossy CGI, no smooth plastic surfaces" · Nijman, [RPS](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/interview-jan-willem-nijman-on-nuclear-thrones-feel) — "It's that attention to details and the relationships of all those systems that matter."

## C. Readability and glanceability for ADHD founders — 10 rules

**C1. Do design for simplicity, clarity, control, support, like this:** one decision per screen, the exit always visible, the time budget always visible ("2 of 3 · ~1 min"), the character available to say the thing again in fewer words.
Source: NN/g, [Neurodivergence: What is it and Why Does it Matter](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/neurodivergence-what-and-why/) — "Neurodivergent users face executive-function, communication, and sensory challenges. Neuroinclusive design helps all users by focusing on simplicity, clarity, control, support, and co-creation." · ADA 2026 Inclusivity finalist [Structured](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/) — "Members of the neurodivergent community have praised Structured for its simple layout".

**C2. Do remove every type style that carries no meaning, like this:** three sizes on a signal screen (headline / text / meta), one italic reserved for Dr HAOS's voice; a fourth size has to earn a named role or it is cut. (The last build's seven sizes were extraneous load by NN/g's own example.)
Source: NN/g, [Minimize Cognitive Load](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/minimize-cognitive-load/) — "Designers should, however, strive to eliminate, or at least minimize, extraneous cognitive load: processing that takes up mental resources, but doesn't actually help users understand the content (for example, different font styles that don't convey any unique meaning)." · "Avoid visual clutter: redundant links, irrelevant images, and meaningless typography flourishes slow users down."

**C3. Do offload reading, remembering and deciding onto the system, like this:** the ℞ is pre-written (the user accepts or skips, never composes); the competitor list is remembered and shown by name on the sheet; the default for every choice is set.
Source: NN/g, [Minimize Cognitive Load](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/minimize-cognitive-load/) — "Offload tasks: Look for anything in your design that requires users to read text, remember information, or make a decision. Then look for alternatives: can you show a picture, re-display previously entered information, or set a smart default?" · "Build on existing mental models … When you use labels and layouts that they've encountered on other websites, you reduce the amount of learning they need to do".

**C4. Do keep body lines at 45–75 characters with 1.5× leading, like this:** at Body 17 on a 393-pt iPhone with 24-pt margins, Geist runs ≈ 42–48 characters per line — do not widen with smaller type to "fit more"; verdicts over 22 words become two sheets.
Source: Baymard, [Readability: The Optimal Line Length](https://baymard.com/research-articles/line-length-readability) — "The optimal line length for body text is 50–75 characters" · "Too narrow: if a line is too short the eye will have to travel back too often, breaking the reader's rhythm." · "lines of text should be 80 or fewer characters" (WCAG 1.4.8) · "Line height (i.e., the spacing between lines of text): 1.5em" · "At the beginning of every new line the reader is focused, but this focus gradually wears off over the duration of the line".

**C5. Do show a beginning and an end, never an infinite list, like this:** the round is 1 → 2 → 3 → discharged; the "all 170" view is paginated by source with a visible count; no feed that loads more at the bottom.
Source: NN/g, [Infinite Scrolling Is Not for Every Website](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/infinite-scrolling/) — "With pagination, there is a beginning and an end. People can anticipate the effort required to scan the page. There is a happy sense of completion when a page is reviewed." · "For task-driven activities, infinite scrolling can feel like drowning in an information abyss with no end in sight." · "People rely on scroll bar to tell them how much effort is left. It's not nice to tell people that they're almost done when they're not."

**C6. Do answer every tap within 0.1 s, show progress after 1 s, and never a static "Loading…", like this:** the sheet lifts on touch-down, not touch-up; a round that takes 2–9 s shows the yellow scan bar sweeping (looped); anything over 10 s shows "reading 120 of 170" on the copier's counter with a Cancel.
Source: NN/g, [Response Times: The 3 Important Limits](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/) — "0.1 second is about the limit for having the user feel that the system is reacting instantaneously" · "1.0 second is about the limit for the user's flow of thought to stay uninterrupted" · "Anything slower than 10 seconds needs a percent-done indicator as well as a clearly signposted way for the user to interrupt the operation." · NN/g, [Progress Indicators](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/) — "Always give some type of immediate feedback." · "Use a progress indicator for any action that takes longer than about 1.0 second." · "For anything that takes less than 1 second to load, it is distracting to use a looped animation" · "Static progress indicators: Don't use them." · "Instead of showing a percentage number, consider showing the number of steps."

**C7. Do let nothing disappear on a timer, like this:** code red stays until tapped; Dr HAOS's line stays until the user moves on; toasts do not exist — the character says it and it stays on the sheet.
Source: [HIG Accessibility — Cognitive](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) — "Minimize use of time-boxed interface elements. Views and controls that auto-dismiss on a timer can be problematic for people who need longer to process information … Prefer dismissing views with an explicit action."

**C8. Do put one interaction on one screen, like this:** signal screen = read + one of {accept ℞, skip}; depth level = read + swipe; competitor add = one field + Done. Nothing asks two things.
Source: [HIG Accessibility — Assistive Access](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) — "Identify the core functionality of your app and consider removing noncritical workflows and UI elements." · "Break up multistep workflows so people can focus on a single interaction per screen." · [Finch critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/) — the failure case: "essential self-care actions visually compete with secondary features."

**C9. Do make the big number big and the text small, and size the touch targets for a thumb, like this:** headline 34–44 pt, body 17, every button 44×44 pt with ≥12 pt around it; red appears on exactly one object (the competitor's bulb and its sheet) and nowhere else, with an icon so it is not colour alone.
Source: [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) — "iOS, iPadOS · Default control size 44x44 pt · Minimum control size 28x28 pt" · "In general, it works well to add about 12 points of padding around elements that include a bezel. For elements without a bezel, about 24 points of padding works well" · "Convey information with more than color alone." · [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars) — "Reserve badges for critical information so you don't dilute their impact and meaning."

**C10. Do stop the user on purpose, like this:** the discharge screen has no "more", no feed below it, one line from Dr HAOS and the time until tomorrow; the app asks for three minutes and then sends the founder away.
Source: Josh Wardle, [NYT interview (Boston.com syndication)](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/) — "It's something that encourages you to spend three minutes a day. And that's it. Like, it doesn't want any more of your time than that." · "There are no ads or flashing banners; no windows pop up or ask for money."

## D. The daily loop, retention without dark patterns — 9 rules

**D1. Do ration the product to one round a day and make scarcity the hook, like this:** three signals, then discharged; no "load three more"; tomorrow's round is announced ("Back at 07:00. I don't sleep.") and cannot be pulled early.
Source: Wardle, [NYT via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/) — "The breakthrough, he said, was limiting players to one game per day. That enforced a sense of scarcity, which he said was partially inspired by the Spelling Bee, which leaves people wanting more" · "While other games send notifications to your phone hoping you'll come back throughout the day, Wordle doesn't want an intense relationship."

**D2. Do open every day with one number and one sentence of what to do, like this:** the morning sheet reads "3 signals · 1 competitor · ~2 min", in the colour of the worst one (teal calm / red code red), and the first tap is the only call to action.
Source: Oura, [Your Oura Readiness Score](https://ouraring.com/blog/readiness-score/) — "Your Readiness Score ranges from 0-100 and tells you, at a glance, if you are ready to face greater challenges or if you need some recovery and rest" · "85 or higher: Optimal … 70-84: Good … Under 70: Pay attention" · "Instead of making you sort through dozens of metrics, Oura gives you three daily scores" · Whoop, [community: What is the Recovery score](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/what-is-the-recovery-score-and-how-is-it-calculated/107) — "A higher Recovery score (green) suggests you're well-rested and ready for intense activity, while a lower score (yellow or red) may indicate fatigue, stress, or poor sleep." and "pushing harder on green days and prioritizing rest on red days".

**D3. Do make the day's completion a visible, closable shape, like this:** three ring segments (one per signal) close as the founder reads; discharge = the ring closed; the Lock Screen widget shows the ring state, not a number of unread.
Source: Apple, [Close Your Rings](https://www.apple.com/watch/close-your-rings/) — "Three rings: Move, Exercise, Stand. One goal: Close them every day. It's such a simple and fun way to live a healthier day that you'll want to do it all the time." · "Choose one of the Activity watch faces or add Activity as a complication to track your progress at a glance throughout the day." · "it gives you a nudge to close your rings at the end of each day."

**D4. Do celebrate the early days disproportionately and change the character on milestones, like this:** day 2 and day 3 get a bigger reaction from Dr HAOS than day 200 ("Two in a row. Don't get used to the praise."); day 7, 30, 100 he physically changes (a coat, a new light) — the "power-up" pattern.
Source: Duolingo, [The habit-building research behind your Duolingo streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/) — "A learner's streak going from 2 to 3 days represents a 50% increase in its length, whereas going from 200 to 201 days is merely an increase of 0.5%!" · "seeing these animations increased the likelihood a brand new learner was still using Duolingo 7 days later by +1.7%" · [You're on fire!](https://blog.duolingo.com/streak-milestone-design-animation/) — "what if we treated milestones like 'power ups' in a video game, and Duo himself physically changed on these exciting days?" · "Timing is everything in animation. Multiple passes of rough animation are done to experiment with different variations on an idea before refining the overall rhythm and energy."

**D5. Do build slack into any streak and never shame a missed day, like this:** a missed day costs nothing visible for two days ("You skipped Tuesday. I noticed. The signals didn't."); no red streak-loss screen; no "are you sure you want to lose…".
Source: Duolingo, [streak research](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/) — "If you lose a day and break your streak, it can have the opposite effect, and actually feel quite demotivating. And the fear of losing a streak could prevent learners from even attempting one in the first place." · "offering people a little 'slack' as they pursue their goals can actually be more motivating than having a rigid set of rules" · "allowing learners to equip up to two Freezes at a time … increased the relative number of active learners on Duolingo every day by +0.38%." · [deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/types/) — Confirmshaming: "The user is emotionally manipulated into doing something that they would not otherwise have done."

**D6. Do reward the rhythm, not the clock, like this:** no bonus for 06:00 readers, no penalty for 22:00 readers; the only thing counted is "read today's round" — the streak is a rhythm counter, not a morning-person test.
Source: The Pokémon Company, [Why We Created "Pokémon Sleep"](https://corporate.pokemon.co.jp/en/topics/detail/110.html) — "We intentionally did not design the app around rewarding specific sleep schedules, such as 'early bird' or 'night owl.' Instead, the app rewards the rhythm and quality of sleep." · "Doing so might encourage users to stay up late or cut their sleep short to catch certain Pokémon." · "Every time you wake up, you can discover how your Pokémon partner slept that night."

**D7. Do send at most one notification a day and make it the content, not an instruction, like this:** "Jungle Scout shipped a cheaper version of your product." — never "Open Nubble to see your signals"; code red may interrupt, routine rounds may not; the badge count is the unread round (0 or 1).
Source: [HIG Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications) — "Provide concise, informative notifications." · "Avoid sending a notification that tells people to perform specific tasks within your app." · "Avoid sending multiple notifications for the same thing" · "Use a badge only to show people how many unread notifications they have." · [deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/types/) — Nagging: "The user tries to do something, but they are persistently interrupted by requests to do something else that may not be in their best interests."

**D8. Do make the share object spoiler-free and in character, like this:** a share card with the day's three sheets as colour blocks and Dr HAOS's one line — no headlines, no competitor names — so the founder can post it without leaking the signal.
Source: Wardle, [NYT via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/) — "he built an automated way for players to brag about their successes in a spoiler-free way." · "it would have looked 'trashy' and not as visually compelling, and he liked the grid's mysterious air" · Duolingo, [streak milestone](https://blog.duolingo.com/streak-milestone-design-animation/) — "a wonderfully simple and attractive share card that allows you to easily save, post, or #humblebrag your achievements".

**D9. Do refuse the named dark patterns by name, like this:** no fake urgency ("only today!"), no fake scarcity, no forced action (no login to read the first round), no hard-to-cancel, no addictive-design loops after discharge. Code red is real urgency with a receipt (competitor, date, URL) or it does not fire.
Source: [deceptive.design taxonomy](https://deceptive.design/types/) — Fake urgency: "The user is pressured into completing an action because they are presented with a fake time limitation." · Fake scarcity: "a fake indication of limited supply or popularity." · Forced action: "The user wants to do something, but they are required to do something else undesirable in return." · Addictive Design: "its design exploits psychological vulnerabilities to foster compulsive behaviour." · HIG [Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts) — "Use alerts to deliver critical — and ideally actionable — information" ([Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)).

## E. Rules we will obey — the per-screen checklist (25)

Tick every line for every screen before it is shown to the owner. A line that cannot be ticked is a bug, not a note.

Navigation and exits
- [ ] 1. An exit is visible without scrolling: Close/Back in the top toolbar (a painted switch is fine, the place is not negotiable), plus swipe-down on modal sheets. (A1, A3, B18)
- [ ] 2. The tab bar is visible unless this screen is a modal; modals never stack (one at a time). (A1, A2)
- [ ] 3. Full-screen is used only for the three-step round; "2 of 3" and the exit stay on screen; no "app within the app". (A2)
- [ ] 4. One interaction per screen; the one action is the only yellow object. (C8, A7)

Type and reading
- [ ] 5. Three type sizes max (headline / text / meta) plus Dr HAOS's italic; no size under 11 pt; body 17 pt; no Light/Thin weights. (A5, C2)
- [ ] 6. At xxxLarge and AX1 the headline is still on top and nothing truncates. (A5)
- [ ] 7. Body lines 45–75 characters; leading ≥1.5×; a verdict longer than 22 words is split. (C4)
- [ ] 8. The big number/headline is readable by someone who knows nothing about the news. (B12)

Colour, contrast, material
- [ ] 9. Text contrast ≥4.5:1 (≤17 pt) or ≥3:1 (≥18 pt / bold), checked in light and dark. (A6)
- [ ] 10. Red appears only on the competitor object, with an icon as well as the colour; nothing else is red. (C9, A7)
- [ ] 11. Liquid Glass only on system controls; the painted scene (floor, wall, machines, sheets, cables) is matte; yellow only on the live machine and its cable, one pink object, red only on code red. (A7, B17)

Motion, depth, feel
- [ ] 12. Every tap reacts within 0.1 s on touch-down (lift/sound/haptic), the cable to what it drives moves first, and the reaction cascades (≥3 channels on the primary action). (C6, B1, B4, B14)
- [ ] 13. Feedback animations ≤300 ms, interruptible, only on meaningful events; no idle motion on everyday buttons. (A8)
- [ ] 14. Reduce Motion variant exists: gyro parallax off, fades instead of slides, no z-depth or blur transitions. (A9)
- [ ] 15. Depth marks hierarchy only (the machine in use forward on the floor); text is flat in the UI layer; every control's cable visibly reaches the thing it changes. (A10, B13)
- [ ] 16. Dr HAOS has idle + reacting + resolution states; he stops when the user moves on; eyes follow the finger ≤15%. (B7, B8)
- [ ] 17. Permanence: what the user did today is still visible on the floor afterwards. (B3)

Haptics and sound
- [ ] 18. Each haptic has one meaning (warning = code red only; rigid = a sheet drops or a switch throws; selection = depth change); a global off switch exists; none fire during the gyro orbit. (A11)
- [ ] 19. Sounds are Ambient-category, <200 ms, obey the silent switch, and are mirrored by a haptic. (A11)

Loading, waiting, stopping
- [ ] 20. Something is on screen within a glance; >1 s shows the yellow scan bar sweeping; >10 s shows "n of 170" plus Cancel; never a static "Loading…". (A12, C6)
- [ ] 21. Nothing auto-dismisses on a timer; no toasts. (C7)
- [ ] 22. The list has an end; no infinite scroll; the round ends in a discharge screen with no "more". (C5, C10, D1)

Loop and honesty
- [ ] 23. The streak (if present on this screen) shows slack, never shame; milestones change the character; day 2–3 get the bigger reaction. (D4, D5)
- [ ] 24. Notifications/badges referenced here: one per day, content not instruction, badge = unread round (0/1); code red carries a receipt. (D7, D9)
- [ ] 25. No alert at launch; alerts only when actionable; no fake urgency, scarcity, forced login or confirmshaming anywhere on the screen. (A12, D9)
